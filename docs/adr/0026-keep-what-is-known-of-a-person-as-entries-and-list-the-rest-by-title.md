# Keep what is known of a person as entries, and list by title what does not come with a thought

A replay of 55 labelled moments from 2026-10-29 to 2026-11-23, each one where answering well needed something from an earlier occasion, asked whether a memory that said it was in view at the time (`replay`, [usage](../usage.md#replaying-memory)). In 17 it came with the thought as development and in 10 it came to mind by itself. In 21 a memory held it but was not in view, though a search for the right words found it; in 2 nothing reached it; in 5 nothing held had recorded it. Of the 27 moments where it was in view the digital life used it in 26, with one unclear. All 13 moments where it missed the earlier thing or got it wrong were among those where it was not in view. What it lacked was not the record but the record in view, and it rarely looked: from 2026-11-05 the thoughts in conversation made 6 deliberate searches in 20 days, and the reviews 180.

Tuning recall did not reach the failures. With the threshold lowered from 0.65 to 0.45 and the limit raised from 3 to 10, 9 of the 23 held memories would have come to mind. Searching with only what had just been said put 7 in the first three, and ranking only the memories of that chat put 5 in the first five. Of the 8 failures among the 23, two would have been reached by any of these. What the failures needed were small standing facts: who a nickname or a running joke means, where someone lives, what someone had asked of it, which model it runs on. Such a fact was a line in an experience of 2,102 characters at the median that cites 21 events, or it had been in an impression and was dropped when the impression was written again. An impression was one entry of at most 600 characters per person, and the 34 people it knew had been written 513 times, one of them 75. The labellers found three facts lost that way, and the digital life later failed on two of them.

What is known of a person is therefore kept as entries, as habits and practices are ([ADR-0025](0025-keep-each-habit-and-practice-as-an-entry-that-says-one-thing.md)): each says one thing about the person or about the two of them, in at most `development.entryChars` characters, rests on its own evidence, and is revised alone. The entries about one person share the scope bound, `development.scopeChars`. Not all of them come with a thought. Those the digital life gave the most importance come whole, the newer first among equals, as far as `development.personChars` reaches, and the first always. The others come as a line each in runtime state, `impressionIndex`, with their title, and `recall` reads one by its ID or everything about a person by scope. Making room for a new fact is then a matter of importance and not of dropping one: a fact that no longer comes whole is a title away. An entry the working context already shows whole stays shown and takes no line in the index ([ADR-0017](0017-tell-the-working-context-each-thing-once.md)).

An index of titles over the experiences themselves was considered and not built. The main group alone has 204 experiences and notes, and their titles name the several unrelated things each one covers, so a list of them says little about where a fact is. Entries by person put the fact where the person is.

The impressions held are split by the one-time split of ADR-0025, which had not yet been deployed and now takes impressions too: 33 of the 34 were already a heading over a numbered list. On a copy of the entries held on 2026-11-25 it split 31 of them, and the 34 people came to 118 entries, 3 a person at the median and 7 at most. Twelve entries are longer than 200 characters and stay as they are until revised. Every person's entries fit within 600 characters, so the index starts empty and fills as more is learned of someone. The program's estimate for all 34 people together rose from 15,558 tokens to 16,975.

The replay gives the same outcomes after this change as before it, moment for moment, which shows only that nothing was lost: at each of those moments an impression was still one entry. Whether small facts now survive, and whether the digital life reads a listed entry when a moment turns on it, can be seen only in moments labelled after a deployment; the replay reports such an entry as `listed`. Two things the replay points to are left for later. Three of the eight failures concerned the digital life itself, which development has no place for. And experiences remain digests of a stretch of chat, which is why what was said so rarely calls the right one to mind.

`development.personChars` used to bound an impression and now bounds how much about a person comes with a thought. This replaces "An impression stays one entry per person under its own bound" in ADR-0025. The instructions and the `remember` and `recall` tools change, so a deploy starts a new working context.
