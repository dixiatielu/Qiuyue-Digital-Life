# Keep pursuits across days and show each life wake how its days have gone

Between 2026-10-28 and 2026-10-29, with autonomous life and fictional daily life both enabled, the digital life set its current activity 20 times. Almost every one was some mix of listening to music, warming up its voice, sorting sheet music, drinking water, and resting. It searched the web once, when someone asked it to, and saved no fictional experience. Its 18 diary notes recorded group chats rather than anything of its own. Three causes stood out. The persona forbade any offline experience, which left only a few activities inside a room. Each life wake started from the same blank prompt, so the model fell back on the most typical activity for a virtual singer. And nothing carried a fictional life from one day to the next: a song practiced in the morning had no progress by the evening.

Continuing state therefore also keeps pursuits ([ADR-0006](0006-experience-understanding-and-continuing-state.md)): current activities that continue across days, each with where it stands now. The glossary already defines a current activity as something being pursued or not yet finished, and the single activity string remains the digital life's current focus. Pursuits are supplied in runtime state with every thought rather than through chance recall, are bounded in number, and are started, advanced, finished, or dropped by the digital life itself while autonomous life mode is enabled. Each pursuit is marked actual or fictional, and one that was ever fictional stays fictional, as with memories ([ADR-0005](0005-configurable-life-with-experience-provenance.md)). A diary entry about a fictional day is likewise saved as fiction whatever its kind, so it needs no actual source and can never become evidence of an actual action.

A life wake also sees its own days. It is given the local date, weekday, and time, because models often misjudge the weekday from an ISO timestamp, and its recent life plans, so that it can see repetition. It is also given one older experience or note and one impression, chosen at random, which it may follow or let pass. The random recollection is deliberate: without some outside variation, the same persona and the same prompt lead to the same choice. The list of actual online activities stays tied to the tools that are available, so adding a platform's tools adds it to the digital life's days without another decision.

Guidance is placed where it applies. The persona file describes the character's life: where it lives, what it likes, what it wants to do, and its flaws. Rules that hold for any persona belong in the instructions, next to the tools they concern: how pursuits and fictional daily life are recorded, that what the digital life says about its own day must agree with its recorded activity and pursuits, and that anything it says it did online must be an action it actually took. Guidance on arranging a day is given in the life wake, at the moment of choosing, instead of in every thought. The program states facts there that a model would otherwise have to infer. These include whether this is the first life wake of the day, with a day starting at 05:00 local time so that staying up past midnight does not start the next one, and, in the evening, whether a diary entry has been written that day. The reminder that fictional daily life brings unplanned events appears only when fictional daily life is enabled. Operator-written persona text cannot depend on that switch.

What kind of life the digital life leads, including whether it has an offline life of its own, belongs to the operator's persona file. This decision supplies only the mechanisms.
