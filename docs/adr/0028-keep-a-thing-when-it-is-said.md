# Keep a thing when it is said, and say what memory is for

The digital life could always save a memory in the middle of a conversation, and almost never did. In the ten days to 2026-11-24, 80 of its 81 habits and practices, 131 of 132 impressions, and 40 of 47 experiences were written during a memory review. In conversation it wrote 20 diary notes, which a life wake asks for in the evening, 7 experiences, one impression, and one habit. The review was added to make up for this, and it does, hours later and from the record alone. A revisit that went back over five old experiences for five facts their moments had needed brought out two ([ADR-0027](0027-go-back-over-old-experiences-with-the-record-beside-them.md)). Yet a thing someone tells it outright, asks of it, or corrects it on needs no hindsight to be worth keeping, and the message that says so is in front of it then.

Three things stood in the way. `remember` required five fields, with source IDs copied exactly, where a reply takes one. A batch of messages ends the thought, but one that also remembered did not, so keeping something cost another model call. And the instructions gave it a sentence, "still use remember", beside paragraphs on the review.

The instructions now say what memory is for: the next time this person, group, or kind of moment comes up, the digital life has only what it kept, so it keeps a thing when it is said, beside whatever it sends; the review comes afterwards and fills in what the whole stretch shows, but sees only the record and can miss what the digital life would have caught. They do not list what to keep. A list of three kinds, what someone says of themselves, asks of it, or corrects, was drafted and dropped: a remark that its way of talking is off would not have been on it, and Anthropic's [The components of agent memory](https://www.anthropic.com/research/components-of-agent-memory) found that a fixed rule about what to record took away much of what stronger models gained from memory. The same article found that weaker models used memory far more when prompted specifically, so the instruction is to be judged by what the digital life then keeps.

Keeping costs less. `remember` needs a kind and the content. In a conversation the sources default to the events the thought is looking at, those from the person or chat the memory is about where there are any; the title defaults to the opening of the content and the importance to 5. A review still gives its sources, since what is in front of it is its own prompt. A habit or impression resting on defaults must still rest on something the digital life did not say itself ([ADR-0012](0012-self-maintained-development-alongside-the-operator-persona.md)). Something remembered beside a message sent ends the thought with it; remembering alone does not, so the reply can follow.

The review's part changes with this. It is told which memories were kept in conversation since the last review and checks them against the record as it reads, correcting or retiring what the record does not bear out; diary notes and fiction are the digital life's own and are not listed. Its prompt also asks it to keep, as an entry of its own, anything the record shows that it would want the next time that person or group comes up. The revisit of ADR-0027 is narrowed to the experiences written before an experience told one thing. Nothing written since can be one of them, so it ends when they are done. A second pass over memories the review had itself just written from the same record, by the same model, was not shown to add anything.

A trial gave the conversation model, gemini-4-hydrogen, four made-up messages on a copy of the database, with sends answered by a stand-in. Told where someone would be working next month, told that its way of ending sentences was tiring, and asked to wait for someone's voice messages to finish, it replied and remembered in the same batch each time, in one model call. Greeted, it replied and kept nothing. It filed the remark on its style under the person who made it, as ADR-0012 has it for what one person asks. Each time it gave the title, importance, and sources itself, so in this trial it was the instruction, not the cost, that had been missing; the defaults stay because they cost nothing. Four messages made to be clear show that it can, not how often it will.

Every `remember` now leaves a `remembered` line in the trace saying whether it came from a conversation or a review and which fields were left to defaults, so the share kept in conversation can be counted before and after a deployment. The conversation model is weaker than the review's and may keep too much; the bounds on entries limit how much, and the review's check is what stands behind their quality.

The instructions and the `remember` tool change, so a deploy starts a new working context.
