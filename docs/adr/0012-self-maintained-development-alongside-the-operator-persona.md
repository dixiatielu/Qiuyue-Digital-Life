# Let the digital life maintain its own development alongside the operator's persona

The glossary already treats the initial persona as a starting point and persona development as change through experience, and [ADR-0003](0003-separate-runtime-control-from-social-requests.md) lets habits, preferences, and relationships change autonomously. There was nowhere to keep that development, however. Between 2026-10-27 and 2026-10-28, 26 memory consolidation prompts produced 11 memories. All of them were experience or diary narratives, and none was a long-term understanding, so what the digital life learned rarely shaped its next thought. When its messages grew far longer than those of the people around it, the operator found and corrected the problem by editing the persona file.

The digital life therefore keeps its own development, separate from the persona file: personal habits (including its own expression style), impressions of the people it knows, and later its practices. The persona file remains the operator's. This includes its sections on behavior and expression style: the digital life does not edit them, and it may only express a wish to change them. Its own habits refine how it lives within that persona, and the persona file takes precedence where the two disagree. It forms and revises its development without operator approval. The operator can inspect, correct, revert, and delete each revision through the management interface, with the same source-dependent deletion as other memories.

Development must rest on external evidence. Each personal habit or impression cites at least one source the digital life did not produce itself: other people's messages, reactions, and recalls; action receipts; or operator corrections. Its own messages and repeated recall of its own judgments cannot support development by themselves. A memory cited as a source carries only the evidence it rests on in turn, so its own notes and earlier judgments are not evidence of their own, and neither are the program's own events, such as wakes and review prompts. This extends the rule in [ADR-0006](0006-experience-understanding-and-continuing-state.md) that recall is not new evidence, and it guards against the self-imitation observed on 2026-10-28, when a working context full of its own long messages kept reinforcing them. What one person asks of the digital life belongs to its impression of that person, not to a habit.

Development is bounded in size, and a revision that would exceed the bound fails so that the digital life merges or drops entries instead of accumulating them. Development that holds everywhere is supplied with every thought, development kept for a group whenever that group appears, and impressions whenever their person appears in the current observation. During a review, the settings and people in the records under review count as present. Like continuing state, none of it depends on a chance retrieval hit. Each entry comes with when it was formed. They are delivered as runtime state rather than in the instructions, so a revision does not discard the working context.

The digital life also learns how the people around it talk and act: message length and rhythm, slang, choice of stickers and reactions, joining chains, and whatever else it notices. The program does not detect these as separate features. Practices record them in the digital life's own words as what people do in which situations, and personal habits record what it decides to adopt. Whether something is a group's norm or one person's way of speaking is the digital life's own judgment, made from the record it reads; the program does not count speakers. A group norm rests on what several people do, excluding accounts the digital life knows to be bots, and one person's way of speaking belongs to the impression of that person. The only mechanical check is the one above: a personal habit or impression cannot rest solely on the digital life's own messages and judgments, because that loop from its own output into memory and back cannot be seen from inside any single step. Habits and practices hold everywhere by default, so what the digital life learns in one setting carries into the others. Only what clearly belongs to one group alone, such as that group's rules, is kept for that group. Knowing a practice does not mean adopting it, and the persona file still takes precedence. Practices that are no longer observed fade.

Reviewing experience for development is part of memory consolidation within the primary thinking process ([ADR-0002](0002-single-attention-with-continuous-perception.md), ADR-0006). Review reads the complete record of a social setting since its last review, looking back at most a day, in order, with the digital life's own messages and others' reactions in place. The program does not select or cut excerpts by content. It renders the record faithfully, remembers how far review has read, and provides tools to page through and search earlier history. Reading on from that point moves it forward. During a review, a jump ahead also moves it forward, because skipping is the digital life's choice; outside review, a lookup moves nothing. A review starts when enough has been experienced since the last one and the digital life is otherwise idle, or sooner if the backlog grows well past that. The digital life decides what is similar, what it missed, and what is worth learning. A review starts with what happened before turning to form: what someone asked of it and whether it did it, where someone corrected, questioned, or contradicted it, said again something it had not taken up, and whether what it said it did or saw matches its actions. It keeps those corrections in what it saves. Its feelings are its own to record, but how someone took its words is only what their replies show, and what the record does not show, including what someone did after their last message, stays unknown. A habit says when it applies and when it does not. Before revising a habit or practice, the review looks at whose lines its examples come from, and one drawn from one person alone moves into the impression of that person, even when it is worded for friends in general. Review failures motivating these rules included unsupported inferences about a participant's state, retaining a claim after a correction, and promoting one person's preferences to global habits. These failure categories can be tested with synthetic conversations; the private source conversations are not part of this design record. Moments when others did something it did not are visible in the complete record without the program having to recognize them. Its existing development carries what earlier reviews learned, so each review revises that understanding instead of rereading all history. A chat record read during a thought is folded out of the working context when that thought ends, and it can be read again. It sits at the end of the context, so the folding leaves the cached prefix before it intact. Unlike agents that fork a background reviewer after each conversation, no second thinking process forms judgments on the digital life's behalf. The operator may separately evaluate persona edits offline by replaying recorded observations. Applying the results is a management change to the persona file, not the digital life's development.

Revision of 2026-11-02: review now runs apart from the conversations, in a working context of its own, while the primary thinking process goes on. This replaces "Reviewing experience for development is part of memory consolidation within the primary thinking process" and "no second thinking process forms judgments on the digital life's behalf" above, and the folding of chat records at the end of a review, which no longer leaves anything to fold in the primary working context. From 2026-10-31 to 2026-11-02, 41 reviews made 406 of 2,908 model calls and read 59 million of 359 million prompt tokens, because each of their calls read the whole working context again, 60,000 to 220,000 tokens of it. Chat records and images were folded when a review ended, but the rest of what it read and wrote stayed: a median of about 25,000 tokens a review and up to 65,000. Of the 36,500 left by the review of 2026-11-02 17:16, recall results took 15,800, runtime data 7,400, and `remember` calls 7,300, and the folded chat records 700. Other events steered into 37 of the 41 reviews, which lasted a median of 4 minutes. None of the 21 messages and stickers sent during them answered the review prompt; they answered messages that had come in meanwhile, decided in a context full of review material. A review that gave way to such events, as idle compaction does, would rarely finish.

The primary working context added little to a review. What a review checks, the chat record and the action receipts, is in the store; thinking is not persisted, and the digital life's own text in the review of 17:16 came to 180 tokens. A fresh context also keeps the review from reading a context full of its own recent messages, the self-imitation this ADR guards against. The reviewer is the same digital life, not a separate judge: it has the same instructions, persona, and development, and its runtime state is refreshed before every call, so a revision made in a conversation reaches a review under way, and a revision made in the review reaches the next call in the conversations ([ADR-0017](0017-tell-the-working-context-each-thing-once.md)). Its context starts with that state and the review prompt and is discarded when the review ends; its exchanges are archived like any other.

A review decides no attention and takes no part in conversations, so the single primary thinking process of [ADR-0002](0002-single-attention-with-continuous-perception.md) is unchanged. New events go to the primary process as before and never steer into a review, which neither waits for the primary process nor gives way to it. A review reads the chat record, events, images, faces, memories, and action outcomes, and writes memories and development. It sends nothing, sets no activity, and plans no life; a commitment it finds still owed it schedules for the primary process to take up when it comes due. Pauses and management changes stop it as they stop the primary process. One review runs at a time, and it still starts when enough has been experienced and the chats are quiet, or sooner once the backlog has grown well past that. Only a review moves the point where review stopped reading a conversation; reading on from that point in a conversation no longer moves it, since a review would then skip lines it had not read.

Both processes may revise the same development. Over the same days, 37 of 162 habits, practices, and impressions were written outside reviews. Revising a version that has already been replaced fails as before, and the failure now names the current version and gives its content, so the reviser merges into it rather than writing over what it added. A review's revision reports what it reached in its result. Where plans, pursuits, or the current activity cite the old version, the revision also comes to the primary process as a `memory_revision` event, since the review cannot revise those itself. What the primary process otherwise learns of a review is the development it revised, through runtime state, and the experiences it saved, through recall.

Second revision of 2026-11-02: a review's revisions that something under way cites now come to the primary process together, in one `memory_revision` event when the review ends, instead of one event each. In the first review run apart, from 18:57 to 19:07, two revisions cited the same pursuit, which like plans and the activity cites every source of the thought that set it ([ADR-0017](0017-tell-the-working-context-each-thing-once.md)), and each started a thought of its own. The event names each plan, pursuit, or activity once. A review that stops before it finishes still hands on what it revised.
