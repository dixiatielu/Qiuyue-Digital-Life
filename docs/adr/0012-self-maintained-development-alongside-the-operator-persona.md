# Let the digital life maintain its own development alongside the operator's persona

The glossary already treats the initial persona as a starting point and persona development as change through experience, and [ADR-0003](0003-separate-runtime-control-from-social-requests.md) lets habits, preferences, and relationships change autonomously. There was nowhere to keep that development, however. Between 2026-10-27 and 2026-10-28, 26 memory consolidation prompts produced 11 memories. All of them were experience or diary narratives, and none was a long-term understanding, so what the digital life learned rarely shaped its next thought. When its messages grew far longer than those of the people around it, the operator found and corrected the problem by editing the persona file.

The digital life therefore keeps its own development, separate from the persona file: personal habits (including its own expression style), impressions of the people it knows, and later its practices. The persona file remains the operator's. This includes its sections on behavior and expression style: the digital life does not edit them, and it may only express a wish to change them. Its own habits refine how it lives within that persona, and the persona file takes precedence where the two disagree. It forms and revises its development without operator approval. The operator can inspect, correct, revert, and delete each revision through the management interface, with the same source-dependent deletion as other memories.

Development must rest on external evidence. Each personal habit or impression cites at least one source the digital life did not produce itself: other people's messages, reactions, and recalls; action receipts; or operator corrections. Its own messages and repeated recall of its own judgments cannot support development by themselves. This extends the rule in [ADR-0006](0006-experience-understanding-and-continuing-state.md) that recall is not new evidence, and it guards against the self-imitation observed on 2026-10-28, when a working context full of its own long messages kept reinforcing them. A habit adopted at one person's request applies by default only within that person's social context.

Development is bounded in size, and a revision that would exceed the bound fails so that the digital life merges or drops entries instead of accumulating them. Personal habits are supplied with every thought, and impressions are supplied whenever their person appears in the current observation. Like continuing state, neither depends on a chance retrieval hit. They are delivered as runtime state rather than in the instructions, so a revision does not discard the working context.

Reviewing experience for development is part of memory consolidation within the primary thinking process ([ADR-0002](0002-single-attention-with-continuous-perception.md), ADR-0006). The program supplies a summary of how others responded to its actions; it does not judge the meaning of that response. Unlike agents that fork a background reviewer after each conversation, no second thinking process forms judgments on the digital life's behalf. The operator may separately evaluate persona edits offline by replaying recorded observations. Applying the results is a management change to the persona file, not the digital life's development.
