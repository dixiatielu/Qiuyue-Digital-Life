# Go back over old experiences with the record beside them

A memory review reads the chat that has come in since the last one ([ADR-0012](0012-self-maintained-development-alongside-the-operator-persona.md)). Nothing read an experience again once it was written. On 2026-11-25 the digital life held 163 experiences, 2,102 characters at the median, each citing 21 events from about an hour of chat; 128 of them were a numbered list, of 8 items at the median. An experience was a digest of a stretch of chat, written minutes after it, before anything showed what in it would matter. The replay of [ADR-0026](0026-keep-what-is-known-of-a-person-as-entries-and-list-the-rest-by-title.md) found the small standing facts its failures needed inside such digests. Splitting a digest does not by itself bring the right one to mind: recall already compares what is said with each line of a memory. What going back can do is put a lasting fact where it is supplied, correct what the record contradicts, and leave an experience that comes to mind as one thing with the events of that thing behind it.

An experience therefore tells one thing that happened, in at most `memory.experienceChars` characters, 600 by default, under a title that names it. A longer one of the digital life's own is refused, and the refusal says to save each thing as its own; a long text of its own is a note. The bound holds for every thought, so no new digests are written.

A memory revisit goes back over those already held. The program picks experiences no review has gone back over and that were written more than a day ago: the ones longer than the bound, and the ones that have come to mind, most often recalled first and then the oldest. It lists each with the stretch of every chat its events come from. The same digital life then reads the memory and that stretch of the record, in a review's working context and with a review's tools, and leaves the memory as it would want to find it: it corrects what the record shows otherwise, keeps each separate thing worth keeping as an experience of its own with the events of that thing as its sources, lets go of the rest, and saves as entries the lasting things it does not already keep about a person or a group. It is given what it already keeps about the people who spoke in those stretches. The program selects and marks; what is worth keeping, and what is lasting, are the digital life's own judgment.

A revisit starts in a quiet moment with no review under way, and only during a rest where the digital life keeps its own days, at most every `memory.revisitEveryHours` hours, 2 by default, for `memory.revisitBatch` experiences, 4 by default; 0 stops it. The batch counts as gone over when the review finishes, so one that stops is taken up again, and what a revisit writes is not gone over a second time.

A revisit departs from a review's revision rules in two ways. Replacing a memory ordinarily withdraws the memories that rest on it, since a correction undoes what was built on the mistake. A revisit changes how a memory is kept more often than what it says, and under that rule a diary entry resting on a replaced digest would leave everyday recall with it; five notes rested on experiences held on 2026-11-25. What rests on a memory a revisit replaces therefore stays, and the result names it so that the digital life can check it. And a review hands the conversations a `memory_revision` event when a plan under way cites what it revised. A plan cites whatever its thought had in view ([ADR-0017](0017-tell-the-working-context-each-thing-once.md)), so a revisit of weeks-old experiences would start a thought in the middle of a rest to say little; the second trial below produced such an event for its one batch. A revisit hands on nothing; its result still lists what cites the old version, and it can schedule a commitment it finds still owed, as a review can.

Two trials ran on a copy of the database with the review model, claude-fable-5-2. In the first, two experiences of 966 and 864 characters became two of 180 and 244 and three entries about people, in 4 model calls. Read against the record, nothing in them was invented; one entry gave the order of two replies wrongly, and the entries retold the occasion under a trait drawn from that one night. The prompt now asks for the lasting thing itself in a sentence, one thing to an entry, and for who said what in the record's order. The second trial took the five experiences that five failed moments of the replay had needed. Their 13,128 characters became 19 experiences of 82 to 306 characters, each citing 6 to 15 events, and 13 entries about people of 38 to 86 characters. Of the five facts those moments needed, two became entries about the person, where someone studies and what someone had asked of it, and three did not: they remain in experiences, now short and titled by what they tell. Five writes were refused for citing the memory being replaced, so the prompt now says its sources are events. That trial made 14 model calls in 525 seconds and read 1.48 million cached input tokens for 33,600 written, about three calls and 300,000 cached tokens an experience. The copy had 116 experiences due.

Notes and diary entries are not gone back over: what the digital life felt is its own to keep, though some hold facts as well. Development entries are not checked against their evidence here; the review that reads new chat revises them. Whether revisits bring what a moment needs into view is for the replay to show on moments labelled after a deployment.

The instructions and the `remember` tool change, so a deploy starts a new working context.

Revision of 2026-11-25: a revisit now takes only the experiences longer than the bound, which are those written before an experience told one thing, and no longer those that have merely come to mind; see [ADR-0028](0028-keep-a-thing-when-it-is-said.md). This replaces "and the ones that have come to mind" above. Among them the most often recalled still go first, and when they are done no revisit starts.

Second revision of 2026-11-25: since going back is done once, it no longer keeps a pace. `memory.revisitEveryHours` is gone and so is the wait for a rest: batch follows batch in any quiet moment with no review under way, a review that is due takes its turn between them, and a batch that stops is tried again after ten minutes. When no experience from before the bound is left the program records that and starts no more. This replaces the paragraph above that begins "A revisit starts in a quiet moment", except that `memory.revisitBatch` still sets the size of a batch and 0 still stops it.
