# Let a model hear and watch what it can, and fold it like an image

A voice message reaches the model as `[voice]` and a video as `[video]`, though the operator's model, gemini-4-hydrogen, takes audio, video, and PDF. Pi's messages hold text and images alone: `MODEL_INPUT_MODALITIES` is `["text", "image"]` in pi-ai 1.4.0 and 1.7.0, and a proposal upstream for audio (earendil-works/pi#11349) was closed and labelled `no-action` in October 2026.

Audio, video, and documents are now **media**: a content block of their own beside text and image, holding a MIME type, the bytes as an image holds them, and how long the media plays or how many pages it has. An observation or a tool result may hold one. It is treated as an image is. It stays in the working context while the thought that perceives it runs, is folded to a note of what it was when the thought ends ([ADR-0024](0024-keep-what-happened-in-the-working-context.md)), never reaches a display, and is archived with its record ([ADR-0004](0004-persist-continuity-beyond-model-context.md)), so whoever attaches media bounds its size. Its cost is counted from its length, 32 tokens a second of audio, 100 a second of video, 560 a page, since Gemini bills by the second and the page; a request's measured usage corrects the estimate. On 2026-11-29 hydrogen reported 214 tokens for about 7 seconds of speech, 520 for 5 seconds of video with sound, and 553 for a one-page PDF.

Media leaves in the form each model's transport takes, at the one place every model call of a thinking process passes. Pi's Google transport sends every block that is not text as `inlineData` under the block's own MIME type, so for `google-generative-ai` and `google-vertex` a media block leaves as an image block of that type. The program's own Interactions transport ([ADR-0023](0023-load-seldom-used-tools-on-demand.md)) sends `audio`, `video`, and `document` content. A model that does not take a kind reads a line in its place, such as `(12s audio omitted: model does not support audio)`, in the words Pi uses for an image a model cannot see. A working context that holds media can therefore go to a review or compaction model of another kind, and across a change of model. Run through the operator's gateway on 2026-11-28 and 2026-11-29, hydrogen repeated a spoken code from MP3 and Ogg Opus, read a code from a PDF, read one from a video's frames and heard its sound track, and heard audio returned by a tool, over both transports; inline videos of 12.5 and 25.7 MB were accepted. The gateway does not expose the Files API (`/v1beta/files` and `/upload/v1beta/files` answered 404), so media goes inline.

What a model takes is declared in `model.input` as `audio`, `video`, and `pdf`, by the operator, for a route where it has been checked, as the flags for adding tools mid-conversation are. Pi's catalog lists text and image for every model, so a catalog model takes `input` too. A declaration no transport can deliver is refused at start rather than left to turn silently into placeholders.

The inline form rests on behaviour Pi does not promise. A test sends media through Pi's own conversion and fails if a later version checks the type of an image block; the remedy then is a transport of ours for `generateContent`, as there is for Interactions.

Considered and set aside:

- Replacing Pi with the Vercel AI SDK, a model catalog from models.dev, and a loop of our own. The SDK replaces only Pi's transports. The loop, catalog, argument validation, overflow detection, and the test provider would become ours to keep, the four transports would be rewritten on it, and hydrogen's `VALIDATED` decoding and byte-stable cache prefix ([ADR-0023](0023-load-seldom-used-tools-on-demand.md)) would need checking again, none of which media needs. [ADR-0009](0009-embedded-pi-with-local-state-and-cli.md) stands.
- Keeping media outside the working context in a cache, with a reference in its place. It spares the archive large records, and adds a cache with a lifetime of its own that forgetting must reach ([ADR-0003](0003-separate-runtime-control-from-social-requests.md)). Bounding the size of what is attached does the first without the second, as it does for images.
- Carrying audio in an image block throughout. It needs no new type, and leaves every reader of the context to tell a picture from a recording by its MIME type.

Not decided here: fetching and transcoding voice and video from a platform, what is attached to an observation unasked and what is viewed on request, and their size limits. A tool for viewing media must be declared whatever the model takes, because the tools a working context declares are part of its identity, and a tool that came and went with the model would begin the context afresh at every change of model. Media a model other than Gemini takes natively, such as a PDF for Claude, needs a transport that sends it.

Revision of 2026-11-29: voice messages and video now reach the model. An adapter says what voice messages, audio, and video an event holds, in the order the event's record numbers them (`[voice 0]`, `[video 1]`), and fetches one when asked. QQ voice is AMR or SILK, which no model takes. SnowLuma's `get_record` converts it on its own server when given `out_format`, so the adapter asks for MP3 and the program carries no converter. QQ video is fetched from the address in its message, and Telegram's Ogg Opus voice and MP4 video through `getFile`, as they are. Among the 60,000 latest events on 2026-11-29 were 11 voice messages and 4 videos, each holding a file name and an address on `multimedia.nt.qq.com.cn` and nothing of its length. The length of an MP3 is therefore read from its bit rate and that of an MP4 from its movie header, within one percent of `ffprobe` on five test files; Telegram gives it.

A voice message is heard with the observation that brings it, by a model that takes audio, up to `limits.maxMedia` in one observation, and not when the account sent it; a minute of speech costs about what an image does. Video and audio files are perceived on request through `view_event_media`, which is declared whatever the model takes, for the reason given above. Nothing over `limits.maxMediaBytes` or longer than `limits.maxMediaSeconds` is fetched for the model.

This replaces "archived with its record" above: the bytes of media are never archived. A video of 12 MB would be 16 MB of text in `context_records` each time one was watched. A saved record holds the note that folding leaves, so a working context restored in the middle of a thought has the note where the media was, and `view_event_media` brings it back. Images are archived as before.

Not checked: `get_record` against the operator's SnowLuma, whose conversion depends on a converter installed there. Left for later: documents and QQ's file messages, which need a download address from another call, and a transcript for a model that takes no audio.

Second revision of 2026-11-29: files reach the model too. Of the 32 file messages among the 400,000 latest events, 14 were PDFs, 6 audio or video, and 2 images, and a model read none of them: an image sent as a file was a name in a line. A QQ file message gives its name and no type, so the name decides. An image is numbered with the message's images and seen as they are, one over 5 MB on request; audio, video, and a PDF are numbered with its media and perceived through `view_event_media`; anything else stays a name. The address a file message carries expires, so a current one is asked for with `get_group_file_url` or `get_private_file_url`, and the message's own is tried when that is refused. Telegram gives a document's type, and a PDF, audio, or video sent as one is perceived the same way.

A PDF is costed by the page, at 1,100 tokens rather than the 560 above: hydrogen reported 17,819 input tokens for a 17-page specification and 103,957 for a 95-page manual, where the nearly blank page measured before had cost 553. Its pages are counted from its page tree, which newer writers keep in compressed object streams, so those are inflated to look; on 31 PDFs found on the development machine the count agreed with `pdfinfo` every time. A document estimated at more than a quarter of `limits.contextTokens` is refused, since no size limit bounds what a page costs. This withdraws documents and file messages from what the first revision left for later. Office and text files are still left: they are not media a model perceives, and reading one is a matter of extracting its text.

Third revision of 2026-11-29: checked against the operator's SnowLuma, which the first revision had not. `get_record` converted four voice messages a day old to MP3 at 128 kbit/s in 0.2 to 0.4 seconds each, and hydrogen heard speech in every one for about what was estimated (319 input tokens for nine seconds, the question included). Files came through `get_group_file_url` and `get_private_file_url`: two images sent as files in a private chat, two videos, and an audio file 19 days old. Three PDFs from groups had the pages `pdfinfo` counts, 2, 322, and 2,178, and a fourth had been deleted from its group.

The address an image or a video message carries lapses within a day. An image 23 hours old answered HTTP 400, as did every older one tried, where one of an hour answered. The message read again with `get_msg` carries a current address, which fetched images up to 351 hours old and a video of 67 hours; videos of 189 hours and more were gone from QQ either way. An image or a video its host refuses is therefore fetched once more from the address `get_msg` gives. Until now `view_event_image` could show an image again only within hours of its being sent.
